## Part I

### Item 1. Business

Meridale Lift Services, Inc. ("Meridale," "we," "us") is the largest independent (non-manufacturer) provider of elevator and escalator services in the United States. We maintain, repair, modernize and monitor vertical transportation equipment made by all major manufacturers. At December 31, 2025, we had approximately 176,300 units under maintenance contract, served from 52 branches in 23 states.

We report four revenue lines:

- **Maintenance services** (55.1% of fiscal 2025 revenue): scheduled maintenance under contracts that typically run five years and renew automatically for further five-year terms unless either party gives notice 90 to 120 days before the renewal date. Contracts are either *standard* (labor and lubricants; parts billed separately) or *comprehensive* (labor and most parts, including work needed to keep equipment in compliance with applicable codes).
- **Repair services** (21.4%): call-back and repair work, billed as performed.
- **Modernization and installation** (20.1%): replacement of controllers, machines, door equipment and cabs, mostly under fixed-price contracts recognized over time.
- **Connected services** (3.4%): LiftSense remote monitoring subscriptions and related hardware.

**Acquisitions.** On March 3, 2025, we acquired Pinecrest Lift Co., a regional service company in the Carolinas and Georgia with about 9,600 units under contract, for US$152.0 million, net of cash acquired. We funded it with cash on hand and borrowings under our revolving credit facility.

**Workforce.** At December 31, 2025, we employed 6,140 people, including 4,380 field technicians. About 79% of our field technicians are covered by the national collective agreement with the International Brotherhood of Lift Mechanics (the "Lift Mechanics"). The current agreement expires June 30, 2026.

**Canada.** We own 50% of Laurent-Meridale Elevateurs S.E.N.C. ("Laurent-Meridale"), which services equipment in Quebec and Ontario. Groupe Laurent Inc. owns the other 50%. We account for it using the equity method.

### Item 1A. Risk Factors (excerpts)

*Changes in elevator codes could require us to perform work at our own cost.* Our comprehensive contracts generally require us to keep covered equipment in compliance with applicable codes. Contract forms used before 2019 do not exclude upgrades required by codes adopted during the contract term. Several states are considering new retrofit requirements for door-lock monitoring and car door restrictors. If adopted in states where we have many comprehensive contracts on older forms, we could incur significant costs that we may not be able to recover through pricing.

*We participate in a multiemployer pension plan that is underfunded.* We contribute to the Lift Mechanics National Pension Fund on behalf of covered employees. If we withdraw from the plan, in full or in part, we would owe withdrawal liability, which could be material. See Note 12.

*Our maintenance revenue depends on contract renewals.* Our contracts renew automatically unless cancelled. Changes in law or practice relating to automatic renewal, notice of renewal or early termination fees could reduce renewals or cancellation fee income.

*Some customer contracts restrict assignment.* About 34% of our maintenance contracts by revenue prohibit assignment without the customer's consent. We do not believe any of these contracts contains a change-of-control provision.

*Labor costs may rise faster than our contract prices.* Most of our maintenance contracts contain annual price escalators, but about 62% of them by revenue cap the annual increase at the lesser of the change in the Consumer Price Index and 4.0%.

#### Critical accounting estimates (excerpts)

*Fixed-price modernization contracts.* We recognize revenue over time based on costs incurred relative to total estimated costs. Changes in estimates are recognized in the period of change. When estimated costs exceed the contract price, we record the full expected loss.

*Capitalized contract acquisition costs.* We capitalize sales commissions paid to obtain maintenance contracts and amortize them on a straight-line basis over the expected period of benefit, which includes expected renewals. **Effective October 1, 2025, we changed the estimated period of benefit from six years to ten years.** The change reflects our analysis of customer relationship duration, including our fiscal 2024 renewal rate of 92.3%, and was applied prospectively as a change in accounting estimate. It reduced amortization expense, recorded in SG&A, by US$1.7 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 (US$1.3 million after tax, US$0.03 per diluted share). We expect it to reduce amortization expense by about US$6.8 million in fiscal 2026. A one-percentage-point change in the long-run renewal rate would change the estimated period of benefit by about one year.

#### Note 11. Income taxes (excerpt)

At December 31, 2025, we had federal net operating loss carryforwards of US$318.0 million (tax-effected US$66.8 million), arising mainly in 2019 to 2021 from our exit from new-equipment installation in the Northeast. Of these, US$61.0 million arose before 2018 and expires from 2034 to 2037; the remainder does not expire.

In November 2023, following the sale of shares by our former largest stockholder, Cinder Bay Partners, and purchases by other holders, we underwent an ownership change under Section 382 of the Internal Revenue Code. As a result, our use of net operating losses that existed at that date is limited to about **US$11.2 million a year**. Any later ownership change would set a new limit, but pre-change losses would remain subject to the lower of the limits that apply to them. We do not expect any of these carryforwards to expire unused, and no valuation allowance has been recorded.

#### Note 12. Multiemployer pension plan

We contribute to the Lift Mechanics National Pension Fund (the "Fund") under our collective agreement. Our contributions were US$41.3 million in 2025 and US$38.9 million in 2024, about 6.1% of total contributions to the Fund. For the plan year beginning January 1, 2025, the Fund was certified in **critical status** and is operating under a rehabilitation plan. The rehabilitation plan raises contribution rates by 5% a year through 2029.

The Fund's actuary estimated that, had we withdrawn completely from the Fund as of January 1, 2025, our withdrawal liability would have been about **US$296 million**. A partial withdrawal, including from ceasing contributions at some facilities or bargaining units, would trigger a proportional liability. We have no current intention to withdraw and have recorded no liability.

#### Note 14. Commitments and contingencies (excerpts)

*Code compliance under comprehensive contracts.* At December 31, 2025, about 68,400 units were under comprehensive contracts, including 22,600 in Illinois and New Jersey, most of them on contract forms used before 2019. In October 2025, Illinois enacted the Elevator Door Integrity Act, which requires door-lock monitoring and car door restrictors on passenger elevators by December 31, 2028. New Jersey's Division of Codes and Standards has proposed a similar rule. We recognize the cost of code-related work under comprehensive contracts as incurred. We are assessing the cost of compliance and our ability to recover it through price adjustments and have not recorded a liability.

*Legacy asbestos claims.* Our former subsidiary Meridale Hoist Works manufactured hoisting equipment until 1994. At December 31, 2025, 41 asbestos-related personal injury claims were pending (34 at December 31, 2024 and 29 at December 31, 2023). We have accrued US$7.2 million for pending and projected claims through 2035 and recorded an insurance receivable of US$4.9 million under a coverage-in-place agreement that pays 68% of defense and indemnity costs until aggregate limits are exhausted, which we project in 2029.

*Personal injury litigation.* In September 2025, the estate of a passenger injured in an escalator incident at Harbor Pointe Mall in Scottsdale, Arizona, sued us and the mall owner, Calder Mall Partners, seeking US$85.0 million. Our general liability program carries a US$2.0 million self-insured retention, which we have accrued, and US$150.0 million of insurance above it. The primary and excess carriers have accepted defense of the claim without reservation of rights.

*Vantor Elevator.* In November 2024, Vantor Elevator Co. sued us in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Indiana, alleging misappropriation of trade secrets by former Vantor employees we hired. We believe the claims lack merit.
