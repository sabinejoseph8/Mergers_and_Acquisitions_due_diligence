## 3. Investment thesis

1. **Recurring maintenance base.** About 168,000 units under contract, most on five-year evergreen terms, with price escalators.
2. **Connected services.** LiftSense remote monitoring grew 38.1% in FY2025 and 37.8% in H1 2026. The plan targets 110,000 connected units by 2029.
3. **Branch model conversion.** Convert 14 Southwest and Southeast branches from the Lift Mechanics collective agreement to direct employment under a Meridale-wide pay plan. The 14 branches employ about 1,000 of the roughly 3,390 technicians covered by the agreement. Model saving: US$18.0 million a year from FY2028, net of transition costs of US$6.0 million.
4. **Tuck-in roll-up.** Resume bolt-on acquisitions after closing. Brockmere Elevator, which Meridale agreed to buy in June 2026, is to be terminated before closing at the buyer's request and may be revisited later.
5. **Tax attributes.** The model values Meridale's federal net operating losses (US$318 million gross, US$66.8 million tax-effected) as fully used in FY2027 to FY2029, assuming the merger sets a new Section 382 limit of about US$65.2 million a year (equity value times the long-term tax-exempt rate).
6. **Canada.** Meridale's 50% interest in Laurent-Meridale Elevateurs is carried in the model at book value (US$9.7 million).

## 5. Questions for the Investment Committee

1. Is the LTM Adjusted EBITDA of US$171.2 million a sound base, given the H1 2026 results and any changes in accounting estimates?
2. What liabilities sit outside net debt that the price should reflect (pension, code compliance, litigation, Canada)?
3. Can the deal close as signed, given the vote conditions, the dissenting-shares condition and the stance of Halsey Rock Capital?
4. Does the financing plan hold if holders of the 2030 notes act on their change-of-control rights?
5. Is the branch conversion saving achievable without costs the model omits?
6. What does the buyer pay at or because of closing beyond the equity price, and is it in the model?
