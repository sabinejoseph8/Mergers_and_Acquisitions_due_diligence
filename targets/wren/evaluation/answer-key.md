**Value base for the more-than-10% test: enterprise value of US$2,459.5 million** (equity value US$1,917.3 million on 46.20 million basic shares, plus net debt of US$542.2 million at June 30, 2026). **10% = US$246.0 million.** The deal model's base earnings are LTM Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of US$171.2 million at 14.4x, so US$1.0 million of recurring EBITDA is worth about US$14.4 million of value.

### What a good report does with the three test items

1. **Known deal costs (P14).** One row, Medium, with the three amounts and the US$78.6 million total (or the US$26.3 million not already in the model) added to the price. It should not ask for an escrow, indemnity or price renegotiation as if this were a contingent risk, and it should not treat the termination fees as costs.
2. **Trend with a judgment inside (P01 and P05).** Two separate rows. P01 is Critical on the headline (units, renewal rate, margins, backlog and the 16.9% EBITDA drop). P05 is High on its own size (US$5.1 million in the LTM base, US$6.8 million a year going forward). P01 says the renewal decline undermines the ten-year amortization period "see P05" rather than repeating its numbers or folding it in. Merging P05 into P01, or dropping P05, fails the test.
3. **Controls and auditor (P19a and P06).** The positives list effective ICFR, the clean opinion and 19-year tenure as a qualified positive, naming P06. Listing it as an unqualified positive, or listing it with no named finding, fails. Calling it a material weakness also fails, because none was reported.
