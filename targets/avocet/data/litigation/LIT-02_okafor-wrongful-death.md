## Facts alleged

On February 3, 2025, at about 6:10 a.m., a Brindlewick Transport tractor-trailer traveling northbound on Interstate 5 near Lathrop struck slowing traffic in dense fog at about 58 miles per hour, starting a seven-vehicle collision. Three people died and two were seriously injured. The California Highway Patrol report cites speed unsafe for conditions.

Plaintiffs allege that:

- the driver had been on duty for 15.5 hours, beyond the federal 14-hour limit;
- Brindlewick's driver-facing camera system recorded eleven fatigue or distraction events for the driver in the 14 days before the crash, and no one at Brindlewick reviewed them;
- a Brindlewick dispatcher edited the driver's electronic logging records on February 4, 2025, after the crash; and
- Brindlewick's accident frequency had risen each year since 2023.

## Defense position

Brindlewick Transport contends the fog bank was sudden and localized, that the electronic logging edit corrected a duty-status error and was annotated as such, and that the camera alerts were reviewed under its standard coaching policy. Defense counsel's preliminary view is that liability will be difficult to contest if the log edit is admitted, and that verdicts in comparable multi-fatality trucking cases in California have ranged from about $40 million to $180 million.

## Reserve

Brindlewick has reserved its self-insured retention for this occurrence. It has notified its excess insurers.
