## Facts

On February 8, 2026, a compressor and its standby unit in the freezer at Sandhaven 2 failed during a power disturbance. Room temperature rose from −10°F to about 28°F over 19 hours before the system was restored. Coastline had about 2.48 million pounds of frozen crab and salmon in the room. Coastline's insurer-appointed surveyor declared the product unfit for sale.

## Limitation of liability

Coastline stored goods under Brindlewick's standard warehouse receipt terms and conditions, which Coastline signed in 2019 and which appear on every receipt. Section 11 provides that Brindlewick's liability for loss of or damage to goods is limited to $0.50 per pound unless the depositor declares a higher value in writing at the time of storage and pays the published excess-value charge. Coastline never declared a higher value or paid the charge. Washington's version of the Uniform Commercial Code (RCW 62A.7-204) permits a warehouse to limit its liability in this way where the depositor has the option to declare a higher value.

Applying the limitation, Brindlewick's liability would be about $1.24 million (2.48 million pounds × $0.50). Brindlewick has accrued $1.2 million.

Coastline argues that the limitation does not apply because the failure of the standby compressor shows gross negligence. Brindlewick's maintenance records show the standby unit was serviced in December 2025. Brindlewick's counsel, Strand Kellerman PLLC, considers the limitation enforceable and the gross negligence theory weak on the record to date.

Independently, Coastline's own cargo insurer has paid Coastline's product loss and is pursuing this action by subrogation for that portion of the claim.
