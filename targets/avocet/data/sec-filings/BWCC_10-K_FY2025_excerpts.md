## Item 1. Business (excerpts)

Brindlewick Cold Chain, Inc. ("Brindlewick," "we," "us") provides temperature-controlled logistics to food producers, grocers, food service distributors and life-sciences distributors in the western United States. We report three segments:

- **Warehousing** (55% of 2025 revenue): frozen and chilled storage and handling at 19 public refrigerated warehouses with 1,148,000 pallet positions.
- **Transportation** (32%): refrigerated truckload and dedicated fleet services through Brindlewick Transport LLC, with 1,380 tractors and approximately 2,600 refrigerated trailers at December 31, 2025.
- **Value-added services** (13%): blast freezing, case picking, kitting, labeling and repacking performed inside our warehouses.

In March 2025 we acquired Bayfront Cold Storage (Oakland, California) for $88.0 million in cash. In June 2025 we opened our Calder Valley distribution center in Riverside County, California.

**Markets and competition.** We compete with national and regional refrigerated warehouse operators and with customers' own facilities. Based on industry capacity data, we estimate that we hold approximately 19% of public refrigerated warehouse capacity in the Puget Sound market and approximately 18% in the Northern Central Valley of California. Trelliswood Logistics Corp. and several privately held operators are our principal competitors in both markets.

**Customers and contracts.** We served approximately 1,310 customers in 2025. No customer accounted for more than 6% of revenue, and our ten largest customers accounted for 34.3%. Warehousing agreements typically run one to five years and set storage rates per pallet per month, handling rates per pallet and, for many customers, minimum monthly volume commitments. Agreements representing approximately 22% of 2025 warehousing revenue contain most-favored-customer provisions that require us to extend to the customer any lower storage rate we grant another customer for comparable services and volumes in the same market.

**Energy.** Refrigeration makes electricity our largest warehouse operating cost after labor. We consumed 589 GWh in 2025 at a total cost of approximately $39.5 million. Fixed-price supply agreements entered into in 2021 supplied approximately 70% of that consumption at a weighted average price of approximately $61 per MWh. Those agreements cover 2022 through 2026 and **expire on December 31, 2026**. The balance of our consumption is purchased at utility tariff or index-linked rates.

**Insurance.** We retain the first $5.0 million of each auto liability occurrence and carry excess auto liability insurance of $50.0 million per occurrence and in the aggregate above that retention. We also retain the first $1.0 million of each workers' compensation and cargo claim.

**Employees.** At December 31, 2025 we had 6,610 employees, of whom 4,610 were hourly warehouse employees. None of our employees is represented by a union.

## Item 1A. Risk factors (selected)

**Our two Sandhaven facilities operate on a port concession that expires in 2028.** Sandhaven 1 and Sandhaven 2 are located on land leased from the Sandhaven Harbor District under a concession that expires on September 30, 2028 and contains no renewal option. The facilities are the principal cold storage serving seafood and agricultural exports through the Port of Sandhaven. We are in discussions with the Harbor District about an extension and expect to negotiate one, but the District is evaluating long-term plans for the Terminal 7 uplands. If the concession is not extended, the improvements revert to the Harbor District and we would need to relocate or lose this business.

**Our electricity costs may rise materially when our fixed-price supply agreements expire at the end of 2026.** Wholesale power prices in the Pacific Northwest and California have risen since 2021. We have not yet contracted supply for 2027.

**Our self-insured retentions expose us to large truck accident claims.** Jury verdicts in truck accident cases have increased sharply in recent years, and individual verdicts can exceed available insurance. A claim above our $55.0 million combined retention and excess coverage would be paid from our own resources.

**Most-favored-customer provisions limit our pricing flexibility.** Discounts offered to win new business in a market may have to be extended to existing customers with most-favored-customer rights.

**A change in ownership of Brindlewick could increase our property taxes.** Under California law, a change in control of a legal entity that owns California real property triggers reassessment of that property at current market value. Our owned facility in Fresno and the Brindlewick-owned improvements on our ground-leased Stockton North and Modesto East sites carry assessed values well below market. We estimate that a reassessment following a change in control would increase our annual property tax expense by approximately $4.7 million.

**Our new warehouse management system may not be implemented on time or on budget.** We began deploying ColdTrack, our new warehouse management system, in 2025. Implementation problems could disrupt service to customers.

**We must verify the employment eligibility of our workforce.** We employ a large hourly workforce. Failures in employment eligibility verification could lead to civil penalties and loss of workers.

**We are a defendant in a putative antitrust class action.** See Item 3.

**Our refrigeration infrastructure is aging.** Seven of our facilities operate ammonia refrigeration systems more than 25 years old. Replacement projects are capital intensive.

## Item 3. Legal proceedings

**Antitrust class action.** On June 12, 2025, a putative class of direct purchasers of refrigerated warehousing services filed *In re Refrigerated Warehousing Services Antitrust Litigation*, No. 3:25-cv-04417, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, against Brindlewick, six other operators and Coldmetric Analytics LLC, a benchmarking firm. Plaintiffs allege that the defendants exchanged current storage and handling rates through Coldmetric's benchmarking service and seek treble damages and injunctive relief. Defendants' motion to dismiss is pending. We believe the claims are without merit. We have not accrued a loss because we cannot reasonably estimate one.

**Calder Valley.** On December 4, 2025, a neighborhood association sued us in Riverside County Superior Court seeking to restrict nighttime truck traffic at our Calder Valley distribution center. See Note 12.

**Polar Ridge.** Westmark Grocers has sued Polar Ridge Logistics LLC, a subsidiary we sold to Arcturus Freight Holdings in 2023, and has named Brindlewick as Polar Ridge's former parent. Under the 2023 purchase agreement Arcturus assumed Polar Ridge's liabilities and agreed to indemnify us. Arcturus has accepted our tender of defense.

**Greenfield Produce.** On March 7, 2025, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of California dismissed with prejudice the breach of contract claims brought against us by Greenfield Produce Co. in 2023. No appeal was filed.

### Liquidity and capital resources

In March 2025 we refinanced our credit facilities with a $300.0 million revolving credit facility and a $325.0 million Term Loan A, each maturing in March 2030, and a $720.0 million Term Loan B maturing in March 2032. The Term Loan A amortizes $25.0 million a year; the Term Loan B has no scheduled amortization. All facilities are prepayable at par. At December 31, 2025 we had $70.4 million drawn on the revolver, $1,032.5 million of term loans outstanding, $79.0 million of finance lease liabilities and $81.0 million of cash. **We have no debt maturities before March 2030.** We were in compliance with all covenants.

**Capital expenditures.** Capital expenditures were $172.9 million in 2025: maintenance $34.9 million, growth $120.0 million (principally completion of Calder Valley) and capitalized software $18.0 million. In 2025 we commissioned Holbrook Engineering Group to assess the condition of refrigeration, roofing and floor systems across our network. The assessment, received in October 2025, identified approximately **$96 million of priority 1 and priority 2 replacement projects for 2027 through 2029**, principally compressor, condenser and control replacements at the seven facilities with ammonia systems more than 25 years old. Pending the assessment, we deferred certain non-critical projects, and maintenance capital expenditures were 2.5% of revenue in 2025 compared with 3.3% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2023.

### Critical accounting estimates

**Self-insurance reserves.** Accrued claims and insurance reserves were $46.5 million at December 31, 2025. We accrue expected auto liability and cargo claims for each accident year at a rate of Transportation revenue based on our historical loss experience and actuarial analysis, and adjust reserves for individual claims as they develop. The accrual rate was 6.5% of Transportation revenue in 2025 and 2024. A 10% change in expected ultimate losses for open accident years would change reserves by approximately $4.6 million.

**Lease-end restoration provisions.** We recognize asset retirement obligations when a legal obligation exists and its fair value can be reasonably estimated. For the restoration provisions in our ground and building leases, we have concluded that settlement dates are indeterminate, and fair value cannot be reasonably estimated, because the leases carry renewal options to between 2039 and 2084 and we intend to operate the facilities for the foreseeable future. See Note 12.

## Item 8. Notes to consolidated financial statements (excerpts)

### Note 7. Leases

Operating lease liabilities were $652.0 million at December 31, 2025 ($640.0 million at December 31, 2024). Operating lease cost is included in cost of revenue and selling, general and administrative expense.

**2024 sale-leaseback.** On November 14, 2024, we sold five warehouses (Fife, Wenatchee, Visalia, Medford and Boise) to Ironbridge Industrial REIT for $214.0 million in cash and leased them back under a 20-year master lease with four five-year renewal options. Initial annual rent is $15.6 million, increasing 2.75% a year. The carrying value of the facilities was $168.0 million, and we recognized a gain of $46.0 million in other income in 2024. The master lease is classified as an operating lease.

The purchase agreement grants Brindlewick a right of first offer if Ironbridge sells any of the facilities and an option to repurchase any or all of the facilities during lease years 10 through 15 at their then fair market value, determined by independent appraisal. We concluded that the transfer qualified as a sale because the repurchase price is fair value at the time of exercise and we believe comparable industrial assets are readily available in the marketplace.

### Note 12. Commitments and contingencies

**Lease-end restoration provisions.** Seven of our ground leases require us, at the end of the lease term, to demolish our improvements and return the land graded and clear. Four of our building leases require us to remove refrigeration systems, insulated panel structures and freezer floors and return the premises to dry-warehouse condition. As described under critical accounting estimates, we have not recognized asset retirement obligations for these provisions. A restoration obligation is recognized when we decide to exit a facility or not to exercise a renewal option. An independent estimate prepared by Holbrook Engineering Group in October 2025 placed the **aggregate undiscounted cost of satisfying these restoration provisions at all eleven facilities at approximately $312.0 million** at 2025 prices. Amounts by facility are shown in Item 2.

**Electricity supply agreements.** Our fixed-price electricity supply agreements expire on December 31, 2026. Remaining commitments under these agreements were approximately $25.2 million at December 31, 2025, all payable in 2026.

**Insurance.** See Item 1. Accrued claims and insurance reserves include our estimate of retained losses for a February 3, 2025 multi-vehicle accident on Interstate 5 involving a Brindlewick Transport tractor, which we have reserved up to our $5.0 million retention.

**Calder Valley.** The Calder Valley Neighbors Alliance seeks a preliminary and permanent injunction prohibiting truck arrivals and departures at our Calder Valley facility between 10:00 p.m. and 6:00 a.m. We believe we comply with the facility's land use approvals and intend to defend the action. We have not accrued a loss.
