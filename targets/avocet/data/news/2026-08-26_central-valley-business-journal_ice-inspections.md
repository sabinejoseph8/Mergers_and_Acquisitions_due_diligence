# Immigration audits at Brindlewick warehouses leave night shifts short as peak season nears

**FICTIONAL.** This article and every company, person and event in it are invented.

*Central Valley Business Journal | August 26, 2026 | By Renata Solis*

Night shifts at several Brindlewick Cold Chain warehouses are running short-handed after federal immigration authorities opened employment eligibility audits at six of the company's sites in California and Washington, according to workers and a staffing agency.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement served Notices of Inspection on July 28, requiring the company to produce Forms I-9 for its employees. Brindlewick disclosed the inspections in its quarterly report and said a self-audit had found unresolved documentation discrepancies for about 410 employees.

Two workers at the Stockton North warehouse said dozens of colleagues had stopped coming to work since the notices were posted. "Whole crews are gone," one said. A Lodi staffing agency said Brindlewick had ordered temporary forklift operators for four of its Central Valley sites at rates about 35% above its own payroll cost.

Employment lawyers said civil penalties for paperwork violations typically run from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars per form, and that the larger risk is losing trained workers before the October to December peak, when holiday frozen food volumes are highest. "Replacing a few hundred experienced freezer workers in eight weeks is extremely hard," said Lorena Bautista, an employment attorney in Sacramento.

Brindlewick said it is "cooperating fully" and "complies with all employment eligibility requirements." It declined to say how many employees had left.
