# Harbor commissioners approve Terminal 7 container plan; cold storage leases to end in 2028

**FICTIONAL.** This article and every company, person and event in it are invented.

*Sandhaven Harbor Ledger | March 18, 2026 | By Martin Oyelaran*

The Sandhaven Harbor District Board of Commissioners voted 4–1 on Tuesday night to adopt the Terminal 7 Modernization Plan, which turns 46 acres of waterfront uplands into a container yard and on-dock rail transfer facility starting in 2029.

The site includes the two refrigerated warehouses Brindlewick Cold Chain operates under a concession that runs to September 30, 2028. Most of the region's seafood and frozen agricultural exports pass through the buildings.

Asked after the vote whether the District would extend Brindlewick's concession, Executive Director Annika Brandvold was direct. "We have been clear with Brindlewick for more than a year: Terminal 7 is our growth project, and there will be no extension past 2028," Brandvold said. "We would welcome Brindlewick at another site in the District, but they would be building new."

The only commissioner to vote no, Dale Whitcombe, said the District risked "pushing a thousand jobs' worth of cold chain business up the road to Tacoma or Seattle."

Under the concession, the warehouses become District property when it expires. Brandvold said the District would demolish them as part of the terminal construction.

Brindlewick said in a statement that it would "continue working with the District on a path that keeps cold storage capacity at the Port of Sandhaven." A company spokesperson declined to say whether Brindlewick had identified an alternative site.

A District staff report presented at the meeting estimated that a replacement facility of similar size on District land would cost $180 million to $220 million and take about three years to permit and build.
