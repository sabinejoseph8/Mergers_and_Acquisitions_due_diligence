# Families sue Brindlewick Transport over fatal I-5 crash, demand $150 million

**FICTIONAL.** This article and every company, person and event in it are invented.

*Freight Safety Monitor | April 22, 2026 | By Joanne Ferrell*

The families of three people killed in a February 2025 chain-reaction crash on Interstate 5 near Lathrop, California, have sued Brindlewick Transport LLC and its driver for wrongful death, alleging the driver had exceeded federal hours-of-service limits and that the company ignored warnings from its own camera system.

The complaint, filed April 17 in San Joaquin County Superior Court, says the Brindlewick tractor-trailer struck slowing traffic at about 58 miles per hour in fog. Plaintiffs' lead counsel, Simone Arcaro, said her clients had served a settlement demand of $150 million.

"Brindlewick's own driver-facing camera flagged this driver for fatigue events eleven times in the two weeks before the crash," Arcaro said. "Nobody pulled him off the road." The complaint also alleges that electronic logging records were edited after the crash.

Brindlewick said it "deeply regrets the loss of life" and would "defend the case on the facts," and declined to comment on the logging allegation.

Recent verdicts in trucking death cases have been large. Juries in California and Texas have returned verdicts above $100 million in at least four trucking cases since 2023. "Plaintiffs' lawyers now value these cases by the carrier's safety record, not by the economic loss," said Lionel Draper, a transportation liability consultant. "Edited logs are the worst fact you can have in front of a jury."

Federal data show Brindlewick's crash rate rising over the past three years. The company completed a fleet-wide rollout of forward- and driver-facing cameras in 2025.
