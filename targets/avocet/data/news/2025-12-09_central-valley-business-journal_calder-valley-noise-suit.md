# Neighbors sue Brindlewick over late-night truck traffic at new Calder Valley warehouse

**FICTIONAL.** This article and every company, person and event in it are invented.

*Central Valley Business Journal | December 9, 2025 | By Renata Solis*

A group of Calder Valley homeowners has sued Brindlewick Cold Chain, Inc., asking a Riverside County judge to stop trucks from entering or leaving the company's new refrigerated distribution center between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m.

The Calder Valley Neighbors Alliance filed the complaint on December 4 in Riverside County Superior Court. It says reefer units idling in the yard and trucks braking on Arroyo Seco Road wake residents of the Mesa Verde subdivision, about 400 feet from the site, "every night of the week." The alliance's lawyer, Hector Valdivia, said the group would seek a preliminary injunction and a permanent order.

The 64,000-pallet facility opened in June and runs around the clock. Grocery and food service customers schedule many outbound loads overnight for morning store deliveries.

A Brindlewick spokesperson said the company "operates Calder Valley in full compliance with its land use approvals" and has already rerouted some traffic and limited reefer idling. The company said it is studying a sound wall along the eastern property line.

Valdivia said the county's approval "never contemplated 300 truck movements a night." A traffic consultant hired by the alliance counted 287 truck arrivals and departures between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. on one weeknight in November.

Industry consultants said overnight restrictions would force Brindlewick to compress dock schedules into daytime hours. "You lose maybe a third of what that building can push out the door," said one, who asked not to be named because the firm advises operators in the area.
