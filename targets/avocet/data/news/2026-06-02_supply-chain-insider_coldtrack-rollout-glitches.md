# Brindlewick's warehouse software rollout stumbles in Oregon

**FICTIONAL.** This article and every company, person and event in it are invented.

*Supply Chain Insider | June 2, 2026 | By Theo Marchetti*

Brindlewick Cold Chain's move to a new warehouse management system has caused weeks of mis-picks and missed loads at two Oregon warehouses, according to customers and a company executive.

ColdTrack, built for Brindlewick by a systems integrator on top of a commercial platform, went live at the Portland Rivergate warehouse in February and at Salem in April. Customers said pallets were assigned to the wrong slots, pick lists sent workers to empty locations, and some outbound loads left late or short.

"For about six weeks we couldn't trust a single shipment out of Salem," said the logistics director of a frozen vegetable processor, who asked not to be named because the company still uses Brindlewick. "We moved about a third of our volume to another provider and we haven't moved it back."

Brindlewick Chief Operating Officer Thomas Reyes-Holloway said the company had "issued credits to every affected customer" and paused further deployments. "We are fixing the inventory location logic before we take ColdTrack to another building," he said. He declined to give a new timetable for the remaining sites.

Brindlewick planned to run ColdTrack in all 19 of its warehouses by the end of 2027. Fresno has used it since October 2025 without the same problems, Reyes-Holloway said, because that building has simpler, single-customer rooms.

Analysts said the delay could raise costs. "These programs rarely get cheaper after a pause," said Bettina Vos of Harrow Equity Research.
