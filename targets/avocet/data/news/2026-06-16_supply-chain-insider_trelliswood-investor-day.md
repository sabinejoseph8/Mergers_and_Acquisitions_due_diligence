# Trelliswood lays out "Network 2028": fewer, bigger buildings and acquisitions in its home markets

**FICTIONAL.** This article and every company, person and event in it are invented.

*Supply Chain Insider | June 16, 2026 | By Theo Marchetti*

Trelliswood Logistics Corp. (NYSE: TWLC) told investors on Monday that it wants to grow by buying refrigerated warehouse capacity in its existing markets and then consolidating it into fewer, larger, automated buildings.

At its investor day in Portland, Oregon, Chief Executive Ramona Castellanos said the company's Network 2028 program targets $1 billion of acquisitions over three years, focused on the Pacific Northwest and Northern California. "Density is the whole game in cold storage," Castellanos said. "Where we buy, we consolidate. We move the customers into our automated buildings and hand back the older leased boxes."

Chief Financial Officer Lucas Brenner said acquired leased warehouses in overlapping markets would "typically be exited within 24 months of closing," with synergies from shared labor, power and transport. He said Trelliswood would keep acquired owned facilities and long-dated ground leases where they add capacity it does not have.

Trelliswood operates 23 refrigerated warehouses. Citing Coldmetric Analytics capacity data, the company said it holds about 24% of public refrigerated warehouse capacity in the Puget Sound market and about 21% in the Northern Central Valley.

Asked about antitrust risk in deals within its own markets, Brenner said customers "have plenty of choices, including building their own." Analysts noted that Brindlewick Cold Chain, which competes with Trelliswood in both markets, has struggled with occupancy this year. Castellanos declined to comment on specific targets.
