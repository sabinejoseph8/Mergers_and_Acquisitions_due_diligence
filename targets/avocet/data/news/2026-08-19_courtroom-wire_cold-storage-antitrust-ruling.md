# Judge lets cold storage price-sharing class action proceed against Brindlewick, six others

**FICTIONAL.** This article and every company, person and event in it are invented.

*Courtroom Wire | August 19, 2026 | By Adaeze Nwosu*

A federal judge in San Francisco has refused to dismiss a proposed class action accusing seven refrigerated warehouse operators, including Brindlewick Cold Chain, of using a benchmarking service to share current storage rates.

In an August 14 order, U.S. District Judge Miriam Castellano of the Northern District of California held that the direct-purchaser plaintiffs had plausibly alleged an agreement to exchange competitively sensitive information through Coldmetric Analytics LLC, whose subscribers received weekly reports of rates by market and customer size. "The reports were current, disaggregated, and limited to participating competitors," the judge wrote. The order allows claims for damages from 2019 onward to proceed to discovery.

Plaintiffs estimate overcharges of at least $600 million across all defendants before trebling. The complaint attributes about 8% of class purchases to Brindlewick, the fourth-largest share among the defendants. Under federal antitrust law, damages are trebled and defendants can be held jointly liable for the whole amount.

Northern Frost Storage, the smallest defendant, settled in July for $9.5 million and an agreement to cooperate with plaintiffs.

A motion for class certification is due February 26, 2027. Brindlewick said it "never agreed with anyone on prices" and would continue to defend the case. Coldmetric said it suspended its rate reports in 2025 and is cooperating with the court.
