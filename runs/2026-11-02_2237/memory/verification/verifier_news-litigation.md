### Most important corrections

1. **NEWS-01: move from Critical to High.** The 10% appraisal condition is "for the benefit of Parent only, which Parent may waive" (8-K Item 1.01). So Halsey Rock's demand gives the buyer an option to walk. It does not block closing. If the buyer waives, the appraisal cost is about US$13.2m to US$27.9m (projection), far below 10% of value.
2. **NEWS-11 / NEWS-10: may meet the Critical test.** The analyst did not have Meridale's own exposure. 10-Q Note 11 gives about 22,400 Meridale units in IL and NJ on comprehensive contracts, "most" without code exclusions. At US$11,000 to US$15,000 per unit that is US$246.4m to US$336.0m, or 10.0% to 13.7% of EV and 12.9% to 17.5% of equity (upper bound). There is no accrual, and the proxy projections exclude the cost. Recommend an X- finding that applies the Critical test.
3. **NEWS-03: no longer an allegation.** The DEFM14A confirms a May 22, 2026 indication at US$44.00 to US$46.00, revised to US$39.00 on Aug 20.
4. **NEWS-07: fix the date and the inference.** The change took effect Oct 1, 2025 (10-K), not February 2026. Use the company's own sensitivity: the renewal rate fell 5.4 points from 92.3%, which implies a benefit period of about 4.6 years against the 10 used (inference). The FY2026 benefit is about US$6.8m.
5. **LIT-09: fix the timing.** Counsel's "likely" view is dated September 2026, and the Q2 10-Q said LDs were not probable. The accrual question is strongest for Q3 2026 (US$9.15m through Sep 30), not Q2. The amounts assume all delay is attributable to Meridale.
6. **LIT-01: correct the cap wording.** The gross-negligence carve-out in the contract covers the consequential damages exclusion. That it also removes the LD cap is St. Aldric's allegation, not a contract term.
7. **LIT-04: correct the insurance wording.** Insurance above the SIR is US$150.0m. Only the primary carrier "accepted" the defense. The excess carriers acknowledged notice without a reservation of rights.
