### Findings

#### LIT-01: St. Aldric arbitration: US$38.0m delay claim from the sixth-largest customer
- **Finding:** St. Aldric Health System claims US$38.0 million for delay on a US$61.4 million fixed-price project to modernize 46 elevators. Completion was due March 31, 2026 and is now forecast for February 2027. Meridale's main defense is the contract cap on liquidated damages (25% of price, US$15.4 million) and the exclusion of consequential damages. St. Aldric alleges gross negligence, which would remove both.
- **Evidence:** Filed 2026-07-14, "Amount claimed | US$38.0 million". Claim components: "Liquidated damages through forecast completion (about 320 days at US$50,000, claimed without the cap) | 16.0"; "Temporary patient transport, diverted procedures and lost revenue | 17.7"; "Third-party consultant and completion costs | 4.3". Allegation: "Meridale reassigned its project crew to other jobs in late 2025 and misrepresented progress". Counsel's view (September 2026): gross negligence "possible but less likely than not." Insurance: "Contract delay claims are not covered by Meridale's general liability program."
- **Exposure:** the upper bound is the full claim of US$38.0 million (1.5% of EV, 2.0% of equity value), uninsured. The contractual floor case is LDs up to the cap: 25% x US$61.4m = US$15.35m, shown as US$15.4m. Recomputed: 320 days x US$0.05m = US$16.0m, so the uncapped LD claim is only US$0.6m above the cap. The cap is reached after about 307 days of delay, around early February 2027, which is close to the forecast completion date (inference). What could raise exposure: a gross negligence finding (adds US$17.7m of consequential damages and removes the cap) or further slippage past February 2027. What could lower it: Meridale's defense that St. Aldric caused the delay (late access to elevator banks, two design changes).
- **Non-monetary:** St. Aldric is "Meridale's sixth-largest customer in FY2025, US$14.8 million of revenue". The relationship is at risk. The crew-reassignment allegation, if proven, would reflect on project execution.
- **Trend:** early stage. No ruling yet. The panel is not yet formed (arbitrator selection 2026-11-18). A hearing is unlikely before closing (inference).
- **Source:** `targets/wren/data/litigation/LIT-01_st-aldric-arbitration.md` (header table, "The contract", "The claim", "Meridale's position", "Insurance"); `docket_index.csv` row LIT-01, filed 2026-07-14
- **Severity:** High. A specific indemnity or escrow is warranted for an uninsured claim from a top customer. It is not Critical: the full claim is 1.5% of EV.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The contract terms, claim amount and counsel's view come from the company's own data-room summary. The gross negligence claim is an untested allegation (Low confidence on that point).
- **Deal impact:** valuation (one-off cash cost of up to US$15.4m to US$38.0m), post-closing (customer retention), disclosure.
- **Cross-check:** confirm St. Aldric's revenue rank and amount in `financials/revenue_by_customer.csv`. Check whether the 10-Q or proxy discloses the arbitration (filed after Q2 ended, on 2026-07-14), and the US$5.2m Q2 2026 loss provision. Check whether the loss provision was added back in Adjusted EBITDA.

#### LIT-09: LIT-01 accounting: liquidated damages counsel calls "likely" are not accrued
- **Finding:** outside counsel says exposure to liquidated damages is "likely, at least in part". The amount can be estimated from the contract rate and cap. Yet "No amount is accrued for liquidated damages." Only a US$5.2m contract loss provision (cost overrun) was recorded in Q2 2026. This raises the question of an unrecorded probable liability under ASC 450, possibly from Q2 2026 onward (inference; for the accounting team).
- **Evidence:** "Q2 2026: Meridale recorded a US$5.2 million loss provision on the contract (estimated costs at completion now exceed the price). No amount is accrued for liquidated damages." Also "Outside counsel's preliminary view (September 2026): exposure to liquidated damages 'likely, at least in part'".
- **Exposure (computed from source figures):** LDs at US$50,000 per day from March 31, 2026 total about US$4.55m through June 30, 2026 (91 days) and about US$10.7m through October 31, 2026 (214 days), both under the US$15.4m cap. Reaching 10% of EV (US$246.0m) is not possible within the cap, so this is a quality-of-earnings and restatement-risk point for Q2 and Q3 2026, not a value-breaking one (inference).
- **Source:** `targets/wren/data/litigation/LIT-01_st-aldric-arbitration.md`, "The contract" and "Meridale's position" (Q2 2026 provision; September 2026 counsel view)
- **Severity:** High. A probable, estimable cost is not in the statements, and it affects the H1/LTM 2026 earnings base the deal is priced on.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The facts come from the company's own summary. Whether the cost is "probable" for accounting purposes is my inference from counsel's wording.
- **Deal impact:** valuation (LTM base), disclosure (Q2 and Q3 2026 10-Qs).
- **Cross-check:** check the 10-Q contingencies note for LD accrual language and the date of any disclosure. The financial analyst should check whether the US$5.2m provision or any LD amount is in or excluded from LTM Adjusted EBITDA.

#### LIT-02: Reyes NJ class action: automatic renewals and early termination fees
- **Finding:** a putative class of about 4,100 New Jersey customer accounts seeks US$35.4 million (treble US$11.8 million of alleged cancellation fees), an injunction and attorneys' fees. It alleges that renewals made without the notice required by the NJ Service Contract Renewal Act are voidable, and that the 50%-of-remaining-value termination fee is a penalty. The motion to dismiss will be heard on December 4, 2026, five days before the December 9 stockholder vote.
- **Evidence:** filed 2026-05-19. "US$35.4 million (treble the US$11.8 million of cancellation fees alleged), plus an injunction and attorneys' fees". "Renewals made without the required notice are voidable at the customer's option." "Meridale began sending renewal reminders in New Jersey in March 2025." "No accrual. The company says a range of loss cannot be estimated."
- **Exposure:** the stated damages claim is the remedy sought: US$35.4m upper bound, plus fees (1.4% of EV, 1.8% of equity value). It is below the Critical test. Beyond the damages claim:
  - NJ is "about 9% of maintenance revenue in FY2025 (about US$53 million)". If renewals are voidable, some of that revenue could be lost through cancellations (inference; no source quantifies it).
  - The termination fee revenue stream is itself in question. Company-wide fees were US$9.8m in FY2025 and US$6.2m in H1 2026. H1 2026 alone is 63% of the full FY2025 amount, so fees appear to be rising (inference). If the fee is ruled a penalty, part of this revenue may not recur.
  - "About 71% of company-wide maintenance contracts are on five-year evergreen terms." The theory could be copied in other states with similar laws (inference; no source shows other suits).
- **Non-monetary:** an injunction against enforcing renewals or fees. Customer relationship and reputational harm in NJ.
- **Trend:** no ruling yet. Meridale's own file says reminders began in March 2025, which is consistent with the plaintiff's allegation that none were sent before 2025 (inference). The motion turns on whether the Act applies to commercial owners, which the file calls "contested."
- **Source:** `targets/wren/data/litigation/LIT-02_reyes-class-action.md` (header table; "Allegations"; "Meridale's position and exposure"); `docket_index.csv` row LIT-02, filed 2026-05-19, hearing 2026-12-04
- **Severity:** High. It needs a specific protection (special indemnity or escrow), and the December 4 ruling falls between signing and the vote. It is not Critical: no source lets the voidable-renewal revenue loss be computed, and the stated claim is 1.4% of EV.
- **Confidence:** Low on the merits (untested pleading allegations). High that the claim and the hearing date exist.
- **Deal impact:** valuation (EBITDA quality of termination fees; NJ revenue base), closing (ruling just before the vote), post-closing (contract practices).
- **Cross-check:** the financial analyst should state how much cancellation fee income is in LTM Adjusted EBITDA. Check whether SEC filings disclose the suit (filed after Q1, before the Q2 10-Q) and the "cannot be estimated" position. Look for news on similar suits in other states.

#### LIT-06: Legacy asbestos: claims rising against a reserve based on a 2023 study
- **Finding:** asbestos claims against the former Meridale Hoist Works are rising: 29, then 34, then 41 pending, with new filings of 7, 9 and 12 a year. The US$7.2m reserve for claims through 2035 relies on an actuarial study from October 2023, made before two years of rising claims. Insurance pays 68% only until it is projected to run out in 2029. After that, Meridale pays 100%.
- **Evidence:** "Reserve: US$7.2 million at December 31, 2025, undiscounted ... based on an outside actuarial study dated October 2023." "pays 68% of defense and indemnity until their aggregate limits are exhausted, projected in 2029. The insurance receivable is US$4.9 million." Next trial: "Cook County, Illinois, set for March 8, 2027. Plaintiff's demand: US$4.5 million."
- **Exposure (computed):** gross annual cost (indemnity plus defense) was US$1.1m in 2023, US$1.3m in 2024 and US$1.7m in 2025. The reserve covers 2026 to 2035, which is about US$0.72m a year gross, well below the 2025 run rate (inference). At the 2025 run rate, ten years would cost about US$17.0m gross (projection), against the US$7.2m reserve. The single US$4.5m trial demand is 5 times the US$0.9m of total indemnity paid in 2025. The net reserve after the receivable is US$2.3m. All of these amounts are under 1% of EV.
- **Source:** `targets/wren/data/litigation/LIT-06_legacy-asbestos.md` (claims table; reserve; insurance; next trial); `docket_index.csv` row LIT-06
- **Severity:** Medium. The dated judgment is stale and needs a refreshed actuarial study. The amount is small relative to value.
- **Confidence:** High on the stated figures. Medium on the adequacy gap, which is my inference.
- **Deal impact:** valuation (small liability outside net debt; pertinent to IC question 2), post-closing cash after 2029.
- **Cross-check:** compare with the 10-K asbestos note (reserve, study date, receivable). Ask whether a study updated after October 2023 exists.
