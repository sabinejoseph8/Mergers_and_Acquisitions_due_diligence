1. Executive summary

Bottom line

Proposed recommendation: Do not proceed at the current price of US$41.50 per share. The Investment Committee makes the decision. We suggest re-approval only after a price adjustment or the specific protections in section 6.

The deal is priced at 14.37x LTM Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of US$171.2m (EV US$2,459.5m; equity value US$1,917.3m). Three verified issues together point to about US$370.3m to 499.9m, or US$8.02 to 10.82 per share (19.3% to 26.1% of equity value), that the price does not reflect (indicative projection; the items are ranges and not certain to add):

  • the Illinois and New Jersey door retrofit after 21% tax: US$194.7m to 265.4m (X-06);
  • the normalized earnings gap at the deal multiple: US$117.8m to 176.7m (X-05);
  • deal costs outside the model: US$57.8m (X-11).

On top of these sit deferred NOL value (X-12) and, if the branch conversion goes ahead, a partial pension withdrawal liability of about US$99.7m (X-10). Separately, the largest holder's stated appraisal intent gives Parent a right not to close (X-01), and the notes put leaves an unfunded need of US$404.0m within 30 days after closing (X-04).

In this report, a 5% or 10% value test counts as met when it is met on equity value (the price paid for the shares). EV percentages are also shown.

2. Answers to the six IC questions

Q1. Is LTM Adjusted EBITDA of US$171.2m a sound base?

No. The arithmetic ties (SEC-01 and FIN-01, not verified; the verified X-05 recomputes it), but the base is overstated and the trend is down.

  • Change in accounting estimate. Effective October 1, 2025, the commission amortization period went from six to ten years. The change cut expense by US$1.7m in Q4 2025 and US$3.4m in H1 2026, so US$5.1m sits inside the LTM base (the amortization is not in the D&A add-back). The renewal rate it cited (92.3%) is now 86.9%; on management's own sensitivity the supportable period is about 4.6 years (inference) (X-14).
  • Add-backs that recur. Branch consolidation and ERP costs of US$2.6m are added back as "non-recurring" every year (FIN-05).
  • Probable unrecorded cost. Outside counsel calls St. Aldric liquidated damages "likely, at least in part"; nothing is accrued. About US$4.6m relates to the LTM period (X-08).
  • Out-of-period error. US$4.1m of FY2025 costs were booked in Q1 2026; the LTM effect is nil to +US$4.1m (SEC-04).
  • Normalized base: US$158.9m to 163.0m, or EV of US$2,282.8m to 2,341.7m at the deal's 14.37x, 4.8% to 7.2% below the deal EV (projection). With the new labor contract's US$8.9m incremental year-one cost (SEC-10), the gap is about 10.0% to 12.4% (X-05).
  • Trend. H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA fell 16.9%; units -4.7%; renewal 86.9%; backlog -17.0%; maintenance margin 37.1% to 34.6%; FY2026 guidance midpoint US$195m cut to about US$168m (X-13). The August case needs H2 2026 margins of 17.9% against 16.4% in H1 excluding charges (SEC-61).
  • Disclosure. The organic growth definition was shortened to six months; FY2025 organic growth was 3.3% on the old basis vs 5.4% reported (SEC-03).

Q2. What liabilities sit outside net debt?

  • Code compliance: US$246.4m to 336.0m upper bound for Illinois and New Jersey door retrofits, unrecorded, with restatement risk for FY2025, Q1 and Q2 2026 (inference) (X-06).
  • Pension: complete withdrawal liability US$338m at January 1, 2026, up 14.2%, unrecorded and not triggered (SEC-11); partial withdrawal of about US$99.7m if the branch conversion goes ahead (X-10).
  • Litigation: St. Aldric (US$38.0m claimed; liquidated damages capped at US$15.4m, about US$10.7m accrued in time by October 31) (X-08); New Jersey renewals (US$35.4m sought plus an injunction; state CID) (X-09); legacy asbestos with a reserve based on an October 2023 study while claims rose from 29 to 41 (LIT-06). Merrin is insured (LIT-04).
  • Canada: Groupe Laurent can put its 50% of Laurent-Meridale on a change of control at the greater of fair value and 12.0x EBITDA, a floor of US$53.4m in cash against US$9.7m book (SEC-24).
  • Other: LTE-M hardware replacement about US$5.2m in 2027 (SEC-36).

Q3. Can the deal close as signed?

At risk. Halsey Rock's 5,267,000 shares (11.4%) exceed the 10% dissenting-shares condition (4.62m shares), and it has said it will demand appraisal if the deal is approved. The condition is for Parent only, so Parent can walk or waive (X-01). A waiver means paying a court-set fair value; the proxy's own May indication of US$44.00 to 46.00 and the DCF top of US$46.80 put the uplift at about US$13.2m to 27.9m on Halsey Rock's shares, plus interest (X-03). The vote needs a majority of the 44.77m unaffiliated shares outstanding; Halsey Rock and Bramford hold 17.8% of them, and non-votes count against (X-02). The proxy excerpts do not mention Halsey Rock's October 6 letter (SEC-46). The NJ motion to dismiss is heard December 4, five days before the vote (X-09). HSR has cleared (SEC-38).

Q4. Does the financing hold if 2030 noteholders put?

Not as modeled. The indenture gives a 101% put on the merger with no ratings trigger: US$404.0m plus accrued interest, offered within 30 days after closing. The debt commitment does not fund it, and the notes trade below 101 (97.25% at June 30; 98.5 on October 6). Post-closing cash of US$72.8m plus the US$250.0m revolver (survival not addressed) leaves a gap of about US$81.2m before other closing cash needs (X-04). There is no financing condition, so the buyer carries the risk (SEC-38). Pro forma leverage is 7.7x reported and 8.2x to 8.4x normalized, with interest cover of about 1.6x to 1.9x (projection) (FIN-25).

Q5. Is the branch conversion saving achievable without omitted costs?

Not without a large omitted cost. The 10-K says ceasing contributions at some bargaining units triggers a proportional partial withdrawal liability. On 1,000 of 3,390 covered technicians that is about US$99.7m (inference), 5.5 years of the US$18.0m saving. The conversions from FY2028 fall within the four-year collective agreement from July 1, 2026, and no source says whether it allows them. Technician attrition is 17.1% (X-10). The new agreement itself adds about US$8.9m of cost in year one against escalators capped at 4.0% (SEC-10).

Q6. What does the buyer pay at or because of closing beyond the equity price?

Fixed known deal costs are US$110.1m (4.5% of EV, 5.7% of equity value): award cash-out US$52.3m (in the model), change-in-control severance US$16.8m (expected to become payable, because Parent plans to replace the executive team except Mr. Pell), the Brockmere termination fee US$9.5m if terminated before December 31, 2026, and the Pellham Rowe fee US$31.5m (US$27.0m on closing). US$57.8m is outside the model (X-11). Contingent items: the notes put (US$404.0m, with a US$4.0m premium) (X-04) and the Laurent put (at least US$53.4m) (SEC-24). The deal EV also leaves out the award cash-out: the fully diluted multiple is 14.7x LTM (FIN-28).

5. Cross-source insights

  • The retrofit cost only appears when sources are combined. The 10-Q gives the unit count and contract terms but no cost; the trade press gives the cost per elevator but not Meridale's units. Together they give US$246.4m to 336.0m (X-06), which the fairness DCF also leaves out (SEC-55).
  • The appraisal condition only bites with the news. The filings show the 10% threshold and Halsey Rock's 11.4%; only the news shows its intent. The proxy then confirms the earlier US$44.00 to 46.00 indication that Halsey Rock had cited as rumor (X-01, X-03).
  • St. Aldric's accrual judgment is contradicted by a later view. The 10-Q says liquidated damages are not probable; the company's own litigation summary records counsel's later view that they are "likely, at least in part" (X-08).
  • The NOL limit is corroborated by the deferred tax trend. The 10-K's 2023 ownership change limit (about US$11.2m a year) matches the US$2.4m yearly deferred tax drawdown (X-12).
  • The amortization benefit sits inside Adjusted EBITDA. The filings do not say so; the cash flow statement shows the amortization is netted outside the D&A add-back (X-14).
  • The conversion saving collides with the pension rules. The deal thesis, the 10-K's proportional-liability language and the fund's critical and declining status combine into a US$99.7m omitted cost (X-10).
  • Named parties: St. Aldric is customer #6 (US$14.8m FY2025); Calder Mall Partners, co-defendant in Merrin, is #5 (US$15.4m, the only top-10 decline, timing only); Reyes Property Group and Hollister Towers are not listed top-10 customers (X-16, not verified).

6. Recommended deal protections and price considerations

  1. Price. Re-base on a normalized LTM of US$158.9m to 163.0m (X-05). Deduct the after-tax retrofit range or hold it in escrow (X-06). Add US$57.8m of deal costs outside the model (X-11), the contingent Laurent put (SEC-24) and the NOL present-value loss (X-12).
  2. Special indemnities or escrow: door retrofit (X-06), St. Aldric (X-08), New Jersey renewals (X-09), and accounting matters in modernization (X-07).
  3. Financing: committed backstop for the US$404.0m notes put plus interest, and confirmation that the revolver survives (X-04). Re-size leverage to the normalized base (FIN-25).
  4. Closing conditions and process: a decision on waiving the appraisal condition; a vote solicitation plan; review of a proxy supplement for Halsey Rock's letter (X-01, X-02, SEC-46).
  5. Representations and bring-down: financial statements and internal controls, no antitrust contact (NEWS-16), and no undisclosed code-compliance commitments.
  6. Thesis changes: net the partial withdrawal liability against the conversion saving and confirm the collective agreement allows conversion (X-10); model the labor contract against capped escalators (SEC-10).
  7. Integration: keep the finance team through the first audit (SEC-40); refresh the asbestos study (LIT-06); safety review after the Merrin inspector's finding (LIT-10).

8. Appendix

Methodology

The system has one lead agent and four specialist subagents: SEC filings, news and media, litigation, and financial analysis. A separate citation verifier checks their work. The analysts read only the data room in targets/wren/data/ and are read-only; the lead agent keeps working memory (plan, findings register, open questions, datasets, run log) and writes the report.

  • Iterations: 2 of a budget of 3. Iteration 1 ran all four analysts in parallel. Iteration 2 sent one narrow follow-up to the SEC analyst on questions the filings could still answer for Critical and High items. The loop stopped because the remaining questions need data not in the room.
  • Checkpoint log:
    • Checkpoint 1: plan summary shown in chat; user replied "approve".
    • Checkpoint 2: red flags shown in chat; user replied "proceed".
    • Checkpoint 3: verification results and draft recommendation shown in chat; user replied "approve report".
  • Verification statistics: 126 findings checked in four parallel runs: 78 Verified, 48 Partially verified (rewritten with the verifier's wording), 0 Rejected. Severity changes after verification: NEWS-01 to High; NEWS-09 to Medium; NEWS-23, SEC-52, SEC-57, SEC-66 and X-15 to Low; SEC-46 and SEC-55 to Medium; FIN-29 and FIN-32 to High.
