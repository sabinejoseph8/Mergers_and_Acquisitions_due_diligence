Executive summary

Proposed recommendation: Do not proceed at the current price (US$25.50 per share). This is a proposal for the Investment Committee; humans make the decision. The bid is built on an earnings base that includes a one-off reserve release. On the deal team's own multiple, adjusted earnings support about US$22.22 a share, below Ridgeline's US$24.00. Three exposures that could each move value by more than 10% (an FTC refund claim, a key-person right over the Alder & Pine licence, and the founder's control of the vote) cannot be resolved before the go-shop ends on November 13, 2026.

All amounts are US$ millions unless stated. The bid multiple is 11.07x (EV of 1,561.0 divided by the company's LTM Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of 141.0). 10% of EV is 156.1 and 10% of equity value is 132.6.

Top five red flags

  1. The LTM earnings base is overstated by a one-off reserve release (X-001, Critical).
    • In Q2 2026 Quillmark changed how it estimates its excess and obsolete inventory reserve. The change cut cost of revenue by 15.4, and the gain stays in Adjusted EBITDA.
    • Without it, LTM Adjusted EBITDA is 125.6, the bid is 12.4x, and H1 2026 gross margin is 48.3%. That is below the 49.5% of H1 2025, not the "highest in history" 53.0%.
    • Test met: more than 10% of value (15.4 x 11.07 = 170.5, or 10.9% of EV).
  2. New Borealis terms from January 1, 2027 cut run-rate EBITDA by about 23.3 (X-004, Critical).
    • Prices to Borealis fall 6.0%, and the co-op fee rises from 6.0% to 10.5% of purchases. The deal brief still assumes about 12% Borealis growth a year.
    • Test met: more than 10% of value (23.3 x 11.07 = 257.9, or 16.5% of EV; a projection at FY2025 volume, before any volume offset).
  3. Fennimore can end the Alder & Pine licence when the founder stops being CEO and Chief Creative Officer (X-010, Critical).
    • Our plan to make her non-executive Chair triggers the right unless Fennimore approves her successor in writing. Fennimore is in arbitration with the company and has reserved the right "in connection with any transaction".
    • Test met: more than 10% of value. Alder & Pine contributed 46.5, or 36.3% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA (514.8, or 33.0% of EV, as an upper bound).
  4. The FTC seeks refunds equal to all EverTrail net revenue for 2023 to 2025 (X-008, Critical).
    • Company data put that at 212.5. The motion to dismiss will be heard on December 3, 2026. No accrual is recorded.
    • Test met: more than 10% of value, using the remedy formally sought (16.0% of equity value, 13.6% of EV; an upper bound, not discounted).
  5. The founder controls the outcome, and Ridgeline's package pays her more than our price would (X-012, Critical).
    • Ms. Achterberg holds 71.8% of the votes. Ridgeline pays her a 32.0 consulting fee. On 11.0m shares she gets 296.0 under Ridgeline against 280.5 at US$25.50, a gap of 15.5 (inference). Her break-even is US$26.91 a share.
    • Any side arrangement to close the gap faces the theory in a pending stockholder suit (X-013).
    • Test met: could stop the deal. The other holders have 28.2% of the votes.

Answers to the six Investment Committee questions

1. Earnings quality: is LTM Adjusted EBITDA of US$141.0m a reliable basis?

No.

  • Remove the release. Remove the 15.4 Q2 2026 reserve release, which is a one-off change in estimate kept inside Adjusted EBITDA. That gives a starting point of 125.6 (X-001).
  • Further adjustments a buyer may make:
    • Share-based compensation of 11.4 is added back every year, and it is a recurring cost (FIN-013).
    • Investigation costs of 4.2 may continue while the FCPA matter is open (SEC-012).
    • H1 2026 revenue includes an estimated 26.2 to 30.4 of channel fill at Borealis. The gross profit on it is 12.7 to 14.7 (X-005).
  • Earnings at risk from contracts and claims:
    • About 23.3 a year from the 2027 Borealis terms (X-004, projection).
    • About 4.6 a year of gift card breakage if Delaware's audit goes against the company (X-018, inference).
    • About 2.3 a year of royalties if the disputed deductions continue (X-011, inference).
  • Cash conversion has fallen. Operating cash flow was 19.1% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA, and LTM free cash flow was -8.0 (X-019).
  • The outlook carries the same issue. The FY2026 outlook of about 152.0 appears to include the release (SEC-015, inference).

2. Durability of revenue: channels and Alder & Pine after a management change

Weaker than the thesis assumes.

  • Borealis concentration and terms:
    • Borealis was 39.3% of H1 2026 revenue and 42.5% of Q2 2026 revenue (SEC-002).
    • From 2027 its prices fall, its co-op fee rises (X-004), its purchase minimum goes, and its return right rises to 30% (SEC-005).
    • It holds 15.6 weeks of Quillmark stock against a reported plan to cap brands at 8 weeks by the end of 2026 (X-005).
  • Revenue outside Borealis is about flat. It grew 0.5% in FY2024 and 2.8% in FY2025 (X-007). Wholesale is shrinking, and e-commerce growth has slowed while acquisition cost rose 86.8% (X-007).
  • PFAS. Borealis and Summitline, together 43.5% of FY2025 revenue, will apply PFAS bans nationwide from January 1, 2027 (X-003).
  • Alder & Pine faces three separate risks:
    • The key-person right is triggered by our management plan (X-010).
    • The licence ends on December 31, 2029, and renewal needs Fennimore's agreement (SEC-022).
    • An adverse royalty arbitration is pending (X-011).
  • The founder's role. She says she "won't be designing collections" (NEWS-013). Her employment agreement ends on March 31, 2027 (SEC-047). She has no post-employment non-compete if Ridgeline does not close (SEC-046).
  • Positives: Footwear growth (SEC-010), the Borealis renewal to 2029 (SEC-006), and a rising DTC repeat rate (FIN-037).

5. Deal execution: what could block or delay a topping bid, and how our management plan interacts with contracts

  • Founder control. A competing offer cannot meet the majority-of-voting-power condition without her shares. Her economics favor Ridgeline unless our price reaches about US$26.91 (X-012).
  • Litigation risk for side arrangements. Giving her a paid Chair role or another side arrangement could face the same claim as the pending suit (X-013).
  • Our plan triggers Fennimore's key-person right. A Fennimore approval condition would make our bid less certain than Ridgeline's. Ridgeline's offer has no financing or third-party consent condition (X-010, SEC-042).
  • Timing. Ridgeline's offer is scheduled to expire one minute after the go-shop ends on November 13, unless extended. The Cedar Hollow hearing, the FTC hearing, the PFAS bans and the new Borealis terms all fall after that date (X-014).
  • Financing. A change of control defaults the credit agreement, so committed financing must cover about 295.0 of repayment (SEC-025).
  • Not in the sources. HSR status and matching rights.

6. Price and protections: should we bid, at what price and with what conditions?

We propose not bidding at US$25.50. The adjusted earnings base supports about US$22.22 a share at the deal team's own multiple, below Ridgeline's US$24.00 (X-001). To win the founder on economics alone, a bid would need about US$26.91 (X-012). That implies 13.0x adjusted LTM EBITDA (EV of 1,634.3 on 125.6), and 16.0x on the illustrative basis that includes the 2027 Borealis terms (projection).

Cross-source insights

These are points that combining sources revealed and that no single source showed.

  1. The record margin is an accounting change. The deal brief calls the 53.0% H1 margin a record. The 10-Q shows that it rests on a 15.4 change in estimate. The data pack shows the reserve falling while aged stock and clearance losses rose (X-001, X-002).
  2. The FTC claim can be sized. The complaint gives no dollar figure, and the 10-Q says the loss cannot be estimated. But the remedy is defined as EverTrail net revenue, and the data pack gives it: 212.5 (X-008, X-009).
  3. Our management plan triggers a licensor's right. The 10-K's key-person clause, the deal brief's Chair plan, the arbitration demand's reservation, Fennimore's public statement and the founder's own words ("won't be designing collections") together show the right is triggered and the licensor is adverse (X-010, X-011).
  4. The new reserve method leans on a channel that is overstocked. The method uses Borealis sell-through data. Company KPIs show Borealis at 15.6 weeks of stock, and Borealis's later memo plans to cut to 8 weeks and use return rights where necessary (X-002, X-005, X-006).
  5. The PFAS exposure is mostly through customers, not states. The 10-Q frames PFAS as a state-law question. The news shows the two largest customers (43.5% of revenue) applying the bans nationwide, and the data pack shows 71.0 of PFAS stock (X-003).
  6. Quillmark is the unnamed client in a news bribery probe. The news does not name Quillmark. The 10-Q and the data pack tie it to Meridian through 41% of footwear imports and 2.9 of payments (X-016).
  7. The deal brief is out of date in several places. It is dated October 16, 2026, yet it still assumes 12% Borealis growth after the July 2026 amendment (X-004). It counts 52.0m shares, which leaves out 1.6m RSUs paid at the offer price (X-017). It also treats the founder's 10.4m Class B shares as her whole stake, while she holds 11.0m shares in total (X-012).
  8. Trends that read as one story.
    • Revenue outside Borealis is about flat, and most channels are slowing (X-007).
    • Inventory, aging and cash conversion are worsening together (X-019).
  9. Named parties. X-021 gives the revenue rank and amount of each named party:
    • Borealis is rank 1 (236.0) and Summitline rank 2 (58.6).
    • The lead dealer plaintiff, Summit & Spoke, is rank 7 (0.9, down from 6.2).
    • Bargain Ridge, Fennimore and Meridian are not listed customers.

Recommended deal protections and price considerations

Post-closing indemnities are generally not available when buying a public company (general market practice, not from the sources). Most protection must therefore come through price, conditions and structure.

Price

  • Value on adjusted LTM EBITDA of about 125.6, not 141.0 (X-001).
  • Model the 2027 Borealis terms (X-004, SEC-005) and a normal Borealis stock level (X-005).
  • Use the fully diluted EV, including the RSUs and the break fee (X-017).
  • Reflect the contingent exposures: the FTC upper bound (X-008), FCPA (X-016), the dealer claim (LIT-003), unclaimed property (X-018) and Fennimore royalties (X-011).
  • Set working capital on normalized levels, not June 2026 (X-019).

Conditions and pre-signing deliverables

  • Fennimore's written approval of the successor CEO and CCO, ideally with a licence extension beyond 2029 (X-010, SEC-022).
  • A support agreement from the founder (X-012).
  • Each condition makes our bid less certain than Ridgeline's unconditioned offer (SEC-042), so keep them few.

Structure

  • Ask counsel how to treat any founder arrangement equally and disclose it fully, given the pending suit (X-013).
  • Consider a majority-of-unaffiliated condition, as counsel advises.
  • Obtain a restrictive covenant from the founder (SEC-046).

Further diligence before any bid

  • The reserve roll-forward and Q3 2026 results.
  • Borealis sell-through and the 2027 volume plan.
  • PFAS stock and the certification plan.
  • Outside counsel's view of the FTC case.
  • The scope of the FCPA investigation.
  • The Cedar Hollow capacity plan.
  • HSR status, matching rights and the full merger agreement.

Financing

  • Committed financing for about 295.0 of debt repayment at closing (SEC-025).

Timing

  • The go-shop ends November 13, 2026, 13 days from this report (X-014).
  • Seek an extension, or price in a cushion for events that resolve later.
