### Findings

#### NEWS-001: Borealis plans to cut vendor economics at contract renewal
- **Finding:** Borealis's CFO said on record that brands will fund more marketing, accept lower wholesale prices, and take back more inventory risk through return-to-vendor rights. Pemberton estimates this cuts gross margin on Borealis sales by 4 to 8 points for mid-sized brands. This conflicts with the deal thesis of about 12% a year Borealis growth.
- **Evidence:** "reset vendor economics"; "share the cost of price competitiveness"; Pemberton: the changes "could reduce gross margin on Borealis sales by four to eight percentage points for mid-sized brands."
- **Source:** `targets/plover/data/news/2025-11-12_retail-ledger_borealis-vendor-terms.md` paras 1 to 3 (L7 to L11) (Retail Ledger, Nov 12, 2025)
- **Claim type:** Fact (Borealis's stated intent, on record). The margin estimate is third-party analyst opinion.
- **Severity:** High. Sizing (projection): about one-third of the ~$720.0m FY2026 outlook is about $240.0m of Borealis sales. A 4 to 8 point cut is $9.6m to $19.2m of gross profit, or 6.8% to 13.6% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA of $141.0m. At 11.1x that is $106.6m to $213.1m, or 6.8% to 13.7% of EV of $1,561.0m. I did not rate it Critical because the upper end rests on a generic analyst estimate for "mid-sized brands", not a confirmed Quillmark term.
- **Confidence:** Medium. On-record CFO quotes, but no source states Quillmark-specific terms.
- **Deal impact:** Valuation (margin and growth thesis); post-closing.
- **Cross-check:** The Borealis vendor agreement (10-K material contract exhibit) for renewal economics and return rights. 10-K risk factors on Borealis. In the financials, Borealis channel gross margin by quarter, to see whether any terms have already changed.
